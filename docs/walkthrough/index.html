From scan to corpus: three pages of one Gpura book

A Gpura corpus would store each item as structured, machine-readable files that record the page layout, the logical text and the metadata, with every piece of text tied to its place on the scan. This walkthrough uses three real pages to show what Gpura already publishes, what the current transcription cannot record, and how the corpus would record it.

What Gpura publishes today

Much of the foundation already exists

Gpura already uses open standards for its records and images, and for this book it also produces a formatted transcription that marks some structure. The corpus would build on all of this rather than replace it.

Omeka S · Dublin Core✓ in place

Catalogue record

Title in two scripts, author, translator, publisher, printer, dimensions, holding library, digitization date. JSON-LD at /api/items/38951.

IIIF Presentation 3✓ in place

Manifest and page images

421 canvases, each page served at 2374 × 3495 px through an IIIF Image API (level 2), so any region can be cropped or rotated by URL.

.txt per page✓ in place

OCR text

Linked from each canvas as a supplementing annotation, plus a full-text file. On other items, a disclaimer in the text says it was produced with Gemini 2.0 Flash.

HTML◐ partial

Formatted transcription

Marks running heads, page numbers, section titles, paragraphs, line breaks, figures and footnotes with CSS classes. Useful, but a custom format, and nothing in it points back to the scan.

PAGE-XML / ALTO✗ missing

Where things sit on the page

No coordinates for regions, lines or words, and no distinction between printed and handwritten text.

TEI✗ missing

A standard edition

No standard way to record reader annotations, damage, uncertain readings, original spelling, or figure content.

Printed p. 1 · finding 1

A reader's note becomes part of the chapter title

On the scanView on scan ↗

The printed chapter title ഭൂപ്രകൃതി, with a reader's handwritten "CMS" in blue ink beside it.

Gpura today (.txt and HTML)
<h2 class="section-title">ഭൂപ്രകൃതി CMS</h2>

The handwriting is now part of the title, in search results and in any dataset built from the text.

In the corpusTEI
<head facs="#z17-title">ഭൂപ്രകൃതി</head>
<add hand="#blue-ink" place="margin" facs="#z17-cms">CMS</add>
PAGE-XML
<TextRegion id="z17-title" type="heading" production="printed">…
<TextRegion id="z17-cms" type="marginalia" production="handwritten-cursive">…

The title stays clean. The note is kept, attributed to a hand, and can be shown or hidden.

Printed p. 1 · finding 2

The printed page number is dropped

On the scanView on scan ↗

Page number "1" at the top right. Viewer page 17 is printed page 1, so citations need both numbers.

Gpura today

Neither the .txt nor the HTML for this page records the "1", although the HTML does record page numbers on other pages.

In the corpusTEI
<pb n="1" facs="https://artifacts.gpura.org/public/1971-american-aikyanadinte-charithram/canvas/17"/>
<fw type="pageNum" place="top-right">1</fw>

The printed number and the canvas are linked, so "p. 1" in a citation resolves to the right image.

Printed p. 2 · finding 1

The running head was reworded

On the scanView on scan ↗

The printed running head reads അമേരിക്കൻ ഐക്യനാടിൻറെ ചരിത്രം.

Gpura today (.txt and HTML)
<header class="header">അമേരിക്കൻ ഐക്യനാടുകളുടെ ചരിത്രം</header>

A different word, not a misread letter. Generative OCR can produce fluent text that isn't on the page, and without coordinates nobody can check a line against its image.

In the corpusPAGE-XML
<TextRegion id="z18-head" type="header">
  <Coords points="…"/>
  <TextLine id="l18-0"><Coords points="…"/>
    <TextEquiv><Unicode>അമേരിക്കൻ ഐക്യനാടിൻറെ ചരിത്രം</Unicode></TextEquiv>
  </TextLine>
</TextRegion>

Every line has a box on the image, so a reviewer, or a script, can compare text and image line by line.

Printed p. 2 · finding 2

Text on the damaged edge is given without doubt

On the scanView on scan ↗

The right edge of the leaf is worn and stained. Words at the line ends, such as പേരു്, sit on the damaged area.

Gpura today

The text gives these words like any other. A reader cannot tell which words were read clearly and which were reconstructed.

In the corpusTEI
<damage agent="wear" facs="#z18-edge">
  <unclear reason="damage" cert="medium">പേരു്</unclear>
</damage>

Damage and uncertainty become data. Readers see where to check the image, and a later review can upgrade the reading.

Printed p. 2 · finding 3

Reader underlining leaves no trace

On the scanView on scan ↗

A reader underlined the sentence beginning അമേരിക്കൻ സംസ്കാരം … in ink. There is more underlining further down the page.

Gpura today

Not recorded. That's a sensible default for reading, but the marks are part of this copy's history: what an earlier reader found important.

In the corpusTEI
<handNote xml:id="dark-ink">underlining in dark ink</handNote>
…
<hi rend="underline" hand="#dark-ink">അമേരിക്കൻ സംസ്കാരം …</hi>
PAGE-XML
<GraphicRegion id="z18-ul1" type="handwritten-annotation">…

Each ink gets its own hand. Whether the blue and dark inks are the same reader stays an open question, and the encoding doesn't force an answer.

Printed p. 2 · finding 4

The same printed form is encoded two different ways

On the scanView on scan ↗

The book is printed in the 1971 reformed script, which draws the conjunct ന്റ in the shape ൻറ throughout: യൂറോപ്പിന്റെ, ഇംഗ്ലണ്ടിന്റെ, പട്ടണത്തിന്റെ all look alike on the page. On this line a reader has also inked over ഇംഗ്ലണ്ടിന്റെ അഞ്ചിരട്ടി.

Gpura today
യൂറോപ്പിന്റെ · ഇംഗ്ലണ്ടിന്റെ · പട്ടണത്തിൻറെ

This is not a spelling change. The first two use the standard Unicode sequence (ന ് റ). The third copies the glyph shape with chillu ൻ + റ, a different character sequence. A search for പട്ടണത്തിന്റെ will not find it.

In the corpusTEI
<editorialDecl>
  <normalization><p>Reformed-script ൻറ and ററ shapes are
    encoded ന്റ and റ്റ; the glyph shape is a property
    of the typeface, not the text.</p></normalization>
</editorialDecl>

One documented encoding rule, applied everywhere and checked by script. The look of the 1971 type is recorded once, not reproduced character by character.

Printed p. 3 · finding 1

Handwriting and the running head become one paragraph

On the scanView on scan ↗

A reader wrote what reads as "Trueman Doctrine" (the Truman Doctrine) and "Kennedy" in blue ink across the printed running head ഭൂപ്രകൃതി. The page number "3" is printed at the right, and text from the other side of the leaf shows through.

Gpura today (HTML)
<p class="paragraph">Freeman Doctrow<br/>പ്രകൃതി<br/>Kennedy.</p>

The handwriting is presented as printed body text, the running head lost its first letters, and the page number is missing.

In the corpusTEI
<pb n="3" facs="…/canvas/19"/>
<fw type="header">ഭൂപ്രകൃതി</fw>
<fw type="pageNum">3</fw>
<add hand="#blue-ink" place="top">
  <unclear cert="medium">Trueman Doctrine</unclear> <lb/>Kennedy
</add>

Print, handwriting and page furniture are separated. The uncertain handwritten word is marked as uncertain instead of guessed.

Printed p. 3 · finding 2

A full-page map becomes one generic sentence

On the scanMap, upright ↗Legend ↗

A relief map printed sideways, with Malayalam place labels, a legend of three elevation bands (in അടി, feet), and a sideways caption അമേരിക്കൻ ഐക്യനാടിൻറെ ഭൂപ്രകൃതി. A reader traced part of the coastline in blue ink.

Gpura today (HTML)
<figure class="image"><img alt="This image contains a map with text labels in Malayalam script."></figure>
<hr class="footnote-separator"/>
<aside class="footnote">പ്രസിദ്ധീകരണത്തിൻ്റെ ഭാഗമായ</aside>

Caption, legend and labels are not transcribed, so the map can't be found by searching. The footnote does not exist: a full-resolution check of the page found only print, the reader’s handwriting, and show-through from the other side of the leaf.

In the corpusTEI
<figure facs="#z19-map" rend="rotate(90)">
  <head>അമേരിക്കൻ ഐക്യനാടിൻറെ ഭൂപ്രകൃതി</head>
  <list type="legend">
    <item><unclear>6</unclear>000 അ. മുകളിൽ</item>
    <item>3000 അ. ,,</item>
    <item>1200 അ. ,,</item>
  </list>
  <figDesc>Relief map of the United States; reader's blue-ink tracing along the coast.</figDesc>
</figure>
PAGE-XML
<ImageRegion id="z19-map" orientation="90">…
<TextRegion id="z19-cap" type="caption" orientation="90">…

In the corpus every piece of text must point to a region on the image. Text with no region, like that footnote, fails validation instead of slipping into the record.

The argument in short

Readable text is not yet a faithful record

Checkable

Every line is tied to its place on the scan. Errors like the reworded running head can be found by comparing, not by luck.

Honest about the copy

Print, reader annotations, damage and uncertainty are recorded separately, so nothing is lost and nothing is passed off as print.

Built to last

TEI, PAGE-XML and IIIF are open, documented standards. The corpus outlives any website, viewer or AI model used to make it.

Free to share

The material is in the public domain, so the corpus can be mirrored by libraries and reused for research and Malayalam language technology.

How it could be produced

A pipeline that builds on what exists

Layout

Detect regions and lines on the full-resolution images: text, headings, figures, handwriting.

Text

Re-run recognition per line, or align the existing OCR text to the detected lines.

Review

Check lines against the image; mark damage, uncertainty and reader hands.

TEI edition

Generate TEI from the layout files, reusing structure the HTML already marks.

Publish

Link the files from the existing IIIF manifests; derive plain text and HTML from TEI.

For the group to discuss

Open questions

Re-OCR or align?

Should the corpus re-recognise text with a tool that outputs layout, or align the existing AI-generated text to detected lines?

Reader marks

Transcribe handwritten notes, or only mark that they exist? Underlining is cheap to flag; marginal notes take more effort.

Spelling

Keep only the printed spelling, or add modern spelling alongside it for search?

PAGE-XML or ALTO

PAGE-XML separates printed from handwritten text and handles rotated regions; ALTO is common in libraries. Pick one, or support both.

Where to start

The whole collection, or a pilot set chosen by the team?
