What the comparison means

  • Gpura's current AI OCR reads body text very well. Re-running OCR is not where the value lies.
  • Its failures are structural: a reader's "CMS" fused into the chapter title, handwriting presented as a printed paragraph, a running head reworded, and a footnote on p. 3 that does not exist on the page.
  • Open-source Tesseract is much weaker on this 1971 type, mostly where reader underlines cross the text.
  • So the corpus pipeline should keep the good AI text, align it to detected lines, and add the layout, provenance and review the AI output lacks.

PAGE-XML or ALTO?

  • PAGE-XML kept everything: reading order with unordered groups for marginalia and map labels, printed vs handwritten, rotated regions, links from marks to the lines they touch, and alternative readings with confidence.
  • The ALTO export validates but flattens: word boxes had to be estimated, handwriting becomes a tag, unordered groups and most links vanish, and many alternative readings could not be kept.
  • Recommendation: PAGE-XML as the master layout format, with ALTO generated for partners that need it.

Encoding decisions to agree on

  • The print uses 1971 reformed-script glyph shapes. ൻറ-shaped NTA is encoded ന്റ, and ററ-shaped TTA is encoded റ്റ: the same characters as modern text, so search works.
  • Samvruthokaram is kept as printed (ഉണ്ടു്). Spelling is diplomatic; nothing is modernised.
  • Reader notes read as "Trueman Doctrine" and "Kennedy", with uncertainty marked. Untranscribed map labels are left as gaps, not guessed.
